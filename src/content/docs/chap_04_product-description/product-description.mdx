Product intent (or *product description*) is the process-independent specification of a finished print product from the perspective of the customer or product designer. Every product decision made by the print buyer—such as the finished page dimensions, color requirements, folding pattern, or paper stock—is expressed as product intent.

Manufacturing and equipment-specific details—such as the imposition layout or the dimensions of the raw press sheet—are irrelevant to the print buyer and are instead modeled as process resources in an XJDF Document. The following XML excerpt shows the product description from our earlier folded leaflet example:

```xml title="Example: product description Folded Leaflet"
[...]
<ProductList>
    <Product Amount="2500" DescriptiveName="My Folded Leaflet">
        <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
            <LayoutIntent Dimensions="841.88 595.27"
                Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead"
                FinishedDimensions="280.69 595.27 0.0"/>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="FoldingIntent">
            <FoldingIntent FoldCatalog="F6-2"/>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
            <ColorIntent>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                    Surface="Front"/>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                    Surface="Back"/>
            </ColorIntent>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
            <MediaIntent MediaQuality="135_MATT"/>
        </Intent>
    </Product>
</ProductList>
[...]
```

Using product intents, most standard commercial print products can be specified in fewer than 20 lines of XML. “Product intent provides a description of finished products from the print buyer point of view” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4 "Product Intent"]. **If an optional attribute or sub-element of an intent is omitted (and no default is specified in the specification), its value defaults to “don't care.” Conversely, if an entire `Intent` element for a given product feature is omitted, that feature is not requested.**

Structurally, every specific intent element (such as `MediaIntent` or `LayoutIntent`) is wrapped inside a generic `Intent` element whose `@Name` attribute matches the name of the inner element. The four most common product intents are `LayoutIntent`, `ColorIntent`, `MediaIntent`, and `FoldingIntent`:

* **`LayoutIntent`** “records the size of the finished pages for the product component [...]. It also describes how the finished pages of the product component are to be imaged onto the finished media. The size definition of the finished media describes the size of a sheet that is folded to create a product, not the size of a production sheet” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.11 "LayoutIntent"]:

```xml title="Example: LayoutIntent"
[...]
<Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
    <LayoutIntent Dimensions="841.88 595.27"
        Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead"
        FinishedDimensions="280.69 595.27 0.0"/>
</Intent>
[...]
```

* **`ColorIntent`** “specifies the color and varnishing of the product. Each surface SHALL be specified individually in a SurfaceColor element” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.5 "ColorIntent"]:

```xml title="Example: ColorIntent"
[...]
<Intent Name="ColorIntent">
    <ColorIntent>
        <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
            Surface="Front"/>
        <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
            Surface="Back"/>
    </ColorIntent>
</Intent>
[...]
```

* **`MediaIntent`** “describes the media to be used for the product component. In some cases, the exact identity of the medium is known, while in other cases, the characteristics are described and a particular stock is matched to those characteristics” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.12 "MediaIntent"]. To simplify substrate matching between systems, XJDF introduced the `@MediaQuality` attribute: “Media with the same `@MediaQuality` are identical from the customer point of view. Thus, characteristics such as weight, coatings or recycling percentage are identical whereas lot or sheet dimension may vary based on production or warehousing requirements” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4 Table 4.34: MediaIntent/@MediaQuality]:

```xml title="Example: MediaIntent"
[...]
<Intent Name="MediaIntent">
    <MediaIntent MediaQuality="135_MATT"/>
</Intent>
[...]
```

This abstract way of identifying paper grades is standard practice in Web-To-Print storefronts: a customer selects “135 gsm glossy” or “170 gsm matte” paper without choosing a specific paper mill, lot number, or parent press sheet size. The `@MediaQuality` token captures this customer-facing paper grade directly. Note that the specification does not predefine a fixed list of `@MediaQuality` tokens; the agreed-upon values are configured between the communicating systems (e.g., the webshop and the MIS).

* **`FoldingIntent`** “specifies the straight line folding, creasing and perforating of a product” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.8 "FoldingIntent"]. In the folding example, it references the `"F6-2"` fold scheme:

```xml title="Example: FoldingIntent for Folding"
[...]
<Intent Name="FoldingIntent">
    <FoldingIntent FoldCatalog="F6-2"/>
</Intent>
[...]
```

As noted in its definition, `FoldingIntent` is also used to specify straight-line creasing and perforation—operations that can be performed inline on a standard folding machine by mounting a creasing or perforation wheel on the slitter shaft immediately after the folding plates:

```xml title="Example: FoldingIntent for Perforating"
[...]
<Intent Name="FoldingIntent">
    <FoldingIntent>
        <Perforate StartPosition="0.0 23.43"
                WorkingPath="0.0 565.68"/>
    </FoldingIntent>
</Intent>
[...]
```

Here, the `Perforate` child element specifies a perforation line, where `@StartPosition` defines the starting point and `@WorkingPath` defines the vector relative to `@StartPosition`. Because rotary perforation wheels on a folding machine can only perforate straight lines parallel to the sheet edge across the full length or width of the sheet, either the X or the Y component of `@StartPosition` and `@WorkingPath` is zero. Partial or irregular die-cut perforations and creases cannot be produced by a rotary wheel and are instead modeled using `ShapeCuttingIntent`, which “describes finishing of products with irregular shapes, including diecutting and adding windows to envelopes” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.14 "ShapeCuttingIntent"].

Overall, product intents play a central role in XJDF: they provide essential job context to shop-floor devices and operators, and they enable standardized Web-To-Print and cross-company procurement workflows connecting online storefronts directly to MIS systems.


## Simple Products
Most everyday commercial print jobs are **simple products**—products that consist of a single product part, such as flyers, folded leaflets, posters, or letterheads. Even a self-cover booklet (where the cover and inner pages share the exact same paper stock and finishing) is a simple product and can be represented by a single `Product` element in the `ProductList`.

The examples below illustrate typical configurations for common simple products. Keep in mind that each `Intent` element supports many additional attributes beyond those shown here—for instance, besides `@MediaQuality`, `MediaIntent` also provides explicit attributes such as `@Weight`, `@Thickness`, `@MediaColor`, and `@GrainDirection`. Consulting Chapter 4 of the XJDF Specification alongside these examples is a great way to explore the full range of available product intent attributes.

### Product: Flyers
The following example describes 1,000 DIN A6 flyers printed four-color (CMYK) on both sides on 135 gsm glossy paper:

```xml title="Example: product description - Flyer"
[...]
<Product Amount="1000" DescriptiveName="FLYER A6">
    <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
        <ColorIntent>
            <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                Surface="Front"/>
            <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                Surface="Back"/>
        </ColorIntent>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
        <LayoutIntent FinishedDimensions="297.64 419.53 0.0"
            Pages="2" Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead"/>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
        <MediaIntent MediaQuality="135_GLOSS"/>
    </Intent>
</Product>
[...]
```

The `Product` element specifies the ordered quantity (`@Amount="1000"`) and a human-readable label (`@DescriptiveName="FLYER A6"`), while its `Intent` child elements define the physical characteristics of the flyer:

* `//LayoutIntent/@FinishedDimensions` “specifies the width (X), height (Y) and depth (Z) [...], respectively, of the finished product after all finishing operations, including folding, trimming, etc. If the Z coordinate is 0, it SHALL be ignored” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4 Table 4.33: LayoutIntent/@FinishedDimensions] (in dtp points).
* `//LayoutIntent/@Pages` “specifies the number of finished pages (surfaces) of the product component, including blank pages. This value SHALL be an even number” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4 Table 4.33: LayoutIntent/@Pages].
* `//LayoutIntent/@Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead"` specifies that the front and back pages are “imposed on the front and back sides so that the head (top) of page contents backs up to each other” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.35: Sides (TwoSidedHeadToHead)].
* `//ColorIntent/SurfaceColor/@ColorsUsed` explicitly lists the four process colors (`"Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"`). Even when the front and back sides use the exact same colors, each surface (`"Front"` and `"Back"`) must be declared in its own `SurfaceColor` element.
* `//MediaIntent/@MediaQuality="135_GLOSS"` identifies the agreed-upon 135 gsm glossy paper grade.

### Product: Folded Leaflets
The next example specifies 750 six-panel Z-folded (accordion-folded) leaflets printed in CMYK on both sides on 135 gsm matte paper:

```xml title="Example: product description - Folded Leaflet"
[...]
<Product Amount="750" DescriptiveName="Folded Leaflet 6-Panels">
    <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
        <LayoutIntent Dimensions="841.89 595.28"
            FinishedDimensions="280.63 595.28 0.0" Pages="2"
            Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead"/>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="FoldingIntent">
        <FoldingIntent FoldCatalog="F6-2"/>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
        <ColorIntent>
            <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                Surface="Front"/>
            <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                Surface="Back"/>
        </ColorIntent>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
        <MediaIntent MediaQuality="135_MATT"/>
    </Intent>
</Product>
[...]
```

Compared to the flat A6 flyer, the folded leaflet adds two items:

1. `//LayoutIntent/@Dimensions` specifies the 2D dimensions of the open (unfolded) sheet (`841.89 × 595.28` dtp points, or DIN A4 landscape), while `//LayoutIntent/@FinishedDimensions` specifies the closed (folded) dimensions (`280.63 × 595.28 × 0.0` dtp points). In product descriptions, the Z coordinate of `@FinishedDimensions` is typically set to `0.0` because the exact folded thickness depends on press and folder settings that are not known at order entry.
2. `FoldingIntent` specifies the fold pattern via `@FoldCatalog="F6-2"`, which “describes the folding scheme according to the folding catalog” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4 Table 4.30: FoldingIntent/@FoldingCatalog] (see “Folding Schemes and Pagination” in Chapter 3). `FoldingIntent` can also describe custom fold sequences and fold orientations when needed.

### Product: Banner with Grommets
Banners with grommets (eyelets) are a classic large-format print product. The example below specifies 10 banners measuring 200 cm × 100 cm, printed one-sided in CMYK on a 500 gsm PVC substrate, with reinforced grommets along the top and bottom (long) edges placed 5 cm from the edge at 10 cm center-to-center intervals:

```xml title="Example: product description - Banner with Grommets"
[...]
<Product Amount="10" DescriptiveName="Banner with Grommets">
    <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
        <LayoutIntent FinishedDimensions="5669.29 2834.65 0.0"
            Pages="2" Sides="OneSided"/>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="HoleMakingIntent">
        <HoleMakingIntent>
            <HolePattern Center="141.73 141.73" Extent="56.69 0.0"
                    Shape="Round" Pitch="283.46 0.0" HoleCount="19"
                    Reinforcement="Grommet"
                    CenterReference="TrailingEdge" />
            <HolePattern Center="141.73 2692.91" Extent="56.69 0.0"
                    Shape="Round" Pitch="283.46 0.0" HoleCount="19"
                    Reinforcement="Grommet"
                    CenterReference="TrailingEdge" />
        </HoleMakingIntent>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
        <ColorIntent>
            <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                Surface="Front"/>
        </ColorIntent>
    </Intent>
    <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
        <MediaIntent MediaQuality="500_PVC"/>
    </Intent>
</Product>
[...]
```

Because the banner is printed on one side only, `//LayoutIntent/@Sides` is set to `"OneSided"` (“page contents SHALL be imposed on the front side” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.35: Sides (OneSided)]) and `ColorIntent` declares a single `SurfaceColor` element for `"Front"`. Notice that `//LayoutIntent/@Pages` is still set to `"2"`, because `@Pages` always counts the total number of physical surfaces of a sheet—including unprinted blank backsides—and must be an even number.

The grommets are specified using `HoleMakingIntent`, which provides “information that identifies the type of holemaking operation, or, alternatively, an explicit list of holes” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.9 "HoleMakingIntent"]. Each `HolePattern` element defines a row of holes starting from an initial hole:

* `@Center` specifies the “(x, y) position of the center of the hole” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 8 Table 8.18: HolePattern/@Center] for the first hole in the pattern (`141.73` dtp points = 5 cm from the edges).
* `@Extent` specifies the “size [...] of each hole, in points. If `@Shape` is "Round", only the first entry of `@Extent` is evaluated and defines the hole diameter” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 8 Table 8.18: HolePattern/@Extent].
* `@HoleCount="19"` “specifies the number of consecutive holes and spaces [...]” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 8 Table 8.18: HolePattern/@HoleCount] along the line, while `@Pitch="283.46 0.0"` “represents the distance between the centers of two adjacent holes” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 8 Table 8.18: HolePattern/@Pitch] (`283.46` dtp points = 10 cm horizontally).
* `@Reinforcement="Grommet"` specifies that each hole is reinforced with a metal or plastic grommet.

In addition to explicit coordinate patterns for large-format banners, `HoleMakingIntent` also provides standard catalog codes for common ring-binder hole patterns (such as two-hole or four-hole office punching on letterheads).


## Complex Products
A key design goal of XJDF was to ensure that the same XML structure used to describe simple products can scale seamlessly to complex, multi-part products simply by adding sibling `Product` elements and linking them together. This structural consistency guarantees long-term interface stability even as a print provider's product portfolio grows from simple flyers to complex bound publications and multi-component displays.

A **complex product** is any product composed of two or more distinct product parts—for example:

* A **magazine with a separate cover**, where the cover and body differ in paper stock, color, or finishing (such as a UV gloss coating on the cover).
* A **notepad**, which consists of a specified number of identical printed sheets glued together along one edge (optionally backed by a chipboard backer).

In XJDF, both products are modeled using the exact same pattern: a **root `Product`** (`@IsRoot="true"`) that represents the finished product as a whole, plus one or more **child `Product` elements** (`@IsRoot="false"`) that characterize the individual product parts (such as the cover and body). All root and child `Product` elements sit side by side at the same hierarchy level inside `ProductList`, which keeps parsing and generation logic simple.

The root `Product` links to its child products via one of three intent types that support child product references:

1. **`AssemblingIntent`** “specifies the creation of a composite component by providing page location, position and attachment method of the respective child products that shall be assembled with the parent product” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.3 "AssemblingIntent"].
2. **`BindingIntent`** “specifies the binding intent for an XJDF job using information that identifies the desired type of binding and which sides SHALL be bound” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.4 "BindingIntent"].
3. **`VariableIntent`** “specifies the variations of the content for printed data with variable content such as lottery tickets or direct mail” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.15 "VariableIntent"].

In `BindingIntent` and `VariableIntent`, child products are referenced via the `@ChildRefs` attribute, which holds an ordered whitespace-separated list of `//Product/@ID` values:

```xml title="Example: BindingIntent with ChildRefs"
<Intent Name="BindingIntent">
    <BindingIntent BindingOrder="Collecting" BindingSide="Left"
        BindingType="SaddleStitch" ChildRefs="COVER_ID BODY_ID"/>
</Intent>
```

The order of IDs in `@ChildRefs` is significant: when `@BindingOrder="Collecting"`, the first ID in `@ChildRefs` (`"COVER_ID"`) identifies the outermost component of the saddle-stitched product.

Both the root product and child products may carry an `@Amount` attribute:

* On a **root `Product`**, `@Amount` specifies the total number of finished products ordered by the customer.
* On a **child `Product`**, `@Amount` specifies how many units of that child part are required to assemble **one** unit of the root product (defaulting to `1` if omitted). For example, for an order of 100 notepads with 25 sheets each, the root product has `@Amount="100"` and the child sheet product has `@Amount="25"`, yielding a total production run of 2,500 sheets (`100 × 25`).

### Product: Magazine with Cover
The following example describes 1,000 saddle-stitched DIN A4 magazines (24 pages total) bound on the left edge. The 4-page cover is printed in CMYK on 135 gsm matte paper with a gloss UV varnish on the outside front/back cover, while the 20-page body is printed monochrome black (`"Black"`) on 80 gsm matte paper:

```xml title="Example: product description of a Magazine with Cover"
[...]
<ProductList>
    <Product Amount="1000" DescriptiveName="My Magazine A4"
            IsRoot="true">
        <Intent Name="BindingIntent">
            <BindingIntent BindingOrder="Collecting"
                BindingSide="Left" BindingType="SaddleStitch"
                ChildRefs="COVER-ID BODY-ID"/>
        </Intent>
    </Product>
    <Product ID="COVER-ID" IsRoot="false">
        <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
            <ColorIntent>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                    Coatings="GlossUV" Surface="Front"/>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                    Surface="Back"/>
            </ColorIntent>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
            <LayoutIntent Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead" Pages="4"
                FinishedDimensions="841.88 595.27 0.0"/>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
            <MediaIntent MediaQuality="135_MATT"/>
        </Intent>
    </Product>
    <Product ID="BODY-ID" IsRoot="false">
        <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
            <ColorIntent>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Black" Surface="Front"/>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Black" Surface="Back"/>
            </ColorIntent>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
            <LayoutIntent Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead" Pages="20"
                FinishedDimensions="841.88 595.27 0.0"/>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
            <MediaIntent MediaQuality="80_MATT"/>
        </Intent>
    </Product>
</ProductList>
[...]
```

Here, the `ProductList` contains three `Product` elements: the root product (`@IsRoot="true"`) holding the `BindingIntent` (`@ChildRefs="COVER-ID BODY-ID"`), and the two child products (`@ID="COVER-ID"` and `@ID="BODY-ID"`, both with `@IsRoot="false"`).

On the cover's front surface (`@Surface="Front"`), the UV gloss varnish is requested via `//ColorIntent/SurfaceColor/@Coatings="GlossUV"`. Coatings are “materials usually applied to a full surface on press as a protective or gloss-enhancing layer over ink” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4 Table 4.24: SurfaceColor/@Coatings].

Notice that `@Coatings` is omitted on the cover's back surface (`@Surface="Back"`) as well as on the body pages. Recall the general rule for product intents: **omitting an attribute means “don't care.”** By omitting `@Coatings` on the inside cover, the customer leaves it up to the print provider whether or not to apply a coating there. If a customer explicitly requires the inside cover to remain uncoated (for example, so a postal address can be inkjet-printed or stamped onto it later), `@Coatings="None"` must be specified explicitly. In commercial printing, leaving `@Coatings` unspecified when the customer has no strict preference gives the printer valuable flexibility—for instance, when ganging multiple jobs on a shared press sheet or applying a neutral protective dispersion varnish to speed up drying and prevent scuffing during finishing.

### Product: Notepad
The following example demonstrates how `@Amount` is used on child products to describe 500 DIN A4 notepads, each containing 25 sheets glued along the top edge. Every sheet is printed four-color CMYK on the front side and monochrome black on the back side on 80 gsm offset paper:

```xml title="Example: product description of a Notepad"
[...]
<ProductList>
    <Product Amount="500" DescriptiveName="Notepad A4"
            IsRoot="true">
        <Intent Name="BindingIntent">
            <BindingIntent BindingOrder="Gathering"
                BindingSide="Top" BindingType="EdgeGluing"
                ChildRefs="SHEET_ID" />
        </Intent>
    </Product>
    <Product Amount="25" ID="SHEET_ID" IsRoot="false">
        <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
            <LayoutIntent FinishedDimensions="595.28 841.89 0.0"
                Pages="2" Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead"/>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
            <ColorIntent>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                    Surface="Front"/>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Black" Surface="Back"/>
            </ColorIntent>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
            <MediaIntent MediaQuality="80_OFFSET"/>
        </Intent>
    </Product>
</ProductList>
[...]
```

Here, the root product specifies `@Amount="500"` (500 finished notepads) and references the single child product (`@ChildRefs="SHEET_ID"`), which specifies `@Amount="25"` (25 sheets per notepad, or 12,500 sheets in total). Because the sheets are stacked on top of one another rather than nested, `BindingIntent` sets `@BindingOrder="Gathering"`, `@BindingSide="Top"`, and `@BindingType="EdgeGluing"`—defined as “gluing gathered sheets at one edge of the pile [...]. Products of this type are also referred to as padded” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4 Table 4.10: BindingType]. Other standard `@BindingType` values include `"Ring"`, `"WireComb"`, `"SoftCover"`, and `"HardCover"`.


## Complex Products including Non-Print Product Parts
Particularly in large-format and display printing, printed graphics are frequently combined with non-print hardware components—such as aluminum roll-up cassette housings, beach-flag poles, or display frames.

In XJDF, such hybrid products are also modeled as complex products consisting of three `Product` elements:

1. A child `Product` describing the **printed graphic** (the artwork dimensions, substrate, and colors, excluding internal assembly margins).
2. A child `Product` describing the **non-print hardware component**, which carries only an `@ID` and an `@ExternalID` referencing the item's SKU/article number in the warehouse or ERP system (e.g., SAP).
3. A root `Product` (`@IsRoot="true"`) containing an **`AssemblingIntent`** that combines the printed part with the non-print container: “AssemblingIntent specifies the creation of a composite component by providing page location, position and attachment method of the respective child products that shall be assembled with the parent product. The containing Product SHALL be referenced in `AssemblingIntent/@Container`. Note: The parent product is not identical to this product. For instance an empty envelope is not the same thing as a filled envelope” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.3 "AssemblingIntent"].

### Product: Roll-up Display

The example below specifies 10 roll-up displays measuring 80 cm × 200 cm, printed one-sided in CMYK on a 500 gsm PVC banner substrate and mounted into a pre-manufactured silver aluminum cassette housing pulled from warehouse stock (`@ExternalID="ARTICLE_ID_HOUSING_SILVER"`):

```xml title="Example: product description of a Roll-up Display"
[...]
<ProductList>
    <Product Amount="10" DescriptiveName="Roll-Up Display"
            IsRoot="true">
        <Intent Name="AssemblingIntent">
            <AssemblingIntent Container="HOUSING_ID">
              <AssemblyItem ChildRef="PRINT_ID" />
           </AssemblingIntent>
        </Intent>
    </Product>
    <Product ID="PRINT_ID" IsRoot="false">
        <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
            <LayoutIntent FinishedDimensions="2267.72 5669.29 0.0"
                Pages="2" Sides="OneSided"/>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
            <ColorIntent>
                <SurfaceColor ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                    Surface="Front"/>
            </ColorIntent>
        </Intent>
        <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
            <MediaIntent MediaQuality="500_PVC"/>
        </Intent>
    </Product>
    <Product ID="HOUSING_ID" IsRoot="false"
        ExternalID="ARTICLE_ID_HOUSING_SILVER"/>
</ProductList>
[...]
```

In the root `Product`, `AssemblingIntent` references the aluminum cassette housing via `@Container="HOUSING_ID"`, while each component inserted into or attached to that container is listed in an `AssemblyItem` child element (`@ChildRef="PRINT_ID"`). The `AssemblyItem` element “describes any individual item that is assembled with the main Product” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 4.3.1 "AssemblyItem"]. Notice that `PRINT_ID` describes only the visible customer-facing graphic (`2267.72 × 5669.29` dtp points = 80 cm × 200 cm); internal manufacturing allowances—such as the extra leader strip needed to clamp or glue the banner into the bottom roller mechanism—are added by the MIS or prepress system when generating the production process resources.


## Web-To-Print Job Submission
In real-world integrations, XJDF product descriptions are most commonly used in **Web-To-Print** environments. Because an online storefront or external procurement system typically has no visibility into a print shop's internal equipment or workflow, a Web-To-Print job submission simply combines a process-independent `ProductList` with commercial order resources—such as customer identification (`CustomerInfo`), requested delivery date and priority (`NodeInfo`), delivery address (`DeliveryParams`), and a link to the print-ready artwork (`RunList`). From this submission, the receiving MIS can automatically map the ordered product to an internal production workflow.

In cross-company and Web-To-Print communication, the XJDF Document is typically wrapped inside a **PrintTalk 2.0** business transaction envelope: “PrintTalk is an XML standard that provides a single format for Print Providers to collaboratively communicate Business Transactions and specifications of Print Product both with their Print Buyers and among themselves. Print industry professionals from a wide range of organizations contributed to the design of PrintTalk. Such organizations include eCommerce companies, print Business Management Systems, print providers, and print equipment manufactures” [@printtalk_2015, chap. 1 "Introduction"].

PrintTalk 2.0 and XJDF are designed to work hand in hand, covering both initial order submission and automated order status feedback. The example below shows a complete PrintTalk 2.0 `PurchaseOrder` submitting an order for 1,000 DIN A6 flyers along with customer, scheduling, and artwork references:

```xml title="Example: Web-To-Print Job Submission via PrintTalk and XJDF"
<ptk:PrintTalk xmlns:ptk="http://www.printtalk.org/schema_2_0"
    xmlns="http://www.CIP4.org/JDFSchema_2_0"
    Timestamp="2016-10-18T20:03:38Z" payloadID="5b7b63e8" >
  <ptk:Request>
    <ptk:PurchaseOrder BusinessID="CART-42">
      <XJDF JobID="JOB-42" Types="Product">
          <ProductList>
              <Product Amount="1000" DescriptiveName="FLYER">
                  <Intent Name="ColorIntent">
                      <ColorIntent>
                          <SurfaceColor
                                  ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                                  Surface="Front"/>
                          <SurfaceColor
                                  ColorsUsed="Cyan Magenta Yellow Black"
                                  Surface="Back"/>
                      </ColorIntent>
                  </Intent>
                  <Intent Name="LayoutIntent">
                      <LayoutIntent
                              FinishedDimensions="297.64 419.53 0.0"
                              Pages="2" Sides="TwoSidedHeadToHead"/>
                  </Intent>
                  <Intent Name="MediaIntent">
                      <MediaIntent MediaQuality="135_GLOSS"/>
                  </Intent>
              </Product>
          </ProductList>
          <ResourceSet Name="CustomerInfo">
              <Resource>
                  <CustomerInfo CustomerID="CUSTOMER-ID"/>
              </Resource>
          </ResourceSet>
          <ResourceSet Name="NodeInfo">
              <Resource>
                  <NodeInfo End="2016-10-20T16:00:00Z"
                      JobPriority="80"/>
              </Resource>
          </ResourceSet>
          <ResourceSet Name="RunList">
              <Resource>
                  <RunList>
                      <FileSpec
                          URL="http://example.org/artwork.pdf"/>
                  </RunList>
              </Resource>
          </ResourceSet>
      </XJDF>
    </ptk:PurchaseOrder>
  </ptk:Request>
</ptk:PrintTalk>
```

The PrintTalk envelope consists of the `ptk:PrintTalk` root element, a `ptk:Request` wrapper, and the `ptk:PurchaseOrder` business object (where `//PurchaseOrder/@BusinessID="CART-42"` identifies the e-commerce shopping cart or purchase order). Besides `PurchaseOrder`, PrintTalk defines additional business objects—such as `RFQ` (Request for Quote), `Quote`, and `OrderStatusResponse`—to model commercial transactions between print buyers and print providers (see the PrintTalk Specification at *https://www.cip4.org*).

Inside the embedded `XJDF` element (`@Types="Product"`), the `ProductList` defines the A6 flyer, followed by three `ResourceSet` elements:

* **`CustomerInfo`:** “The CustomerInfo resource contains information about the customer who ordered the job” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 6.24 "CustomerInfo"] (here identifying the account via `@CustomerID="CUSTOMER-ID"`).
* **`NodeInfo`:** “The NodeInfo resource contains information about planned scheduling. It allows the MIS to plan, schedule and invoice jobs or job parts” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 6.60 "NodeInfo"] (here specifying the requested completion deadline `@End` and `@JobPriority="80"`).
* **`RunList`:** References the customer's print-ready PDF artwork via `FileSpec/@URL`.

Additional resources—such as `DeliveryParams` for shipping addresses and delivery instructions, or specific process parameter resources if a customer explicitly requests a special production technique (e.g., a specific screening method)—can be included in the same way.

### Status Feedback in a Web-To-Print Environment
PrintTalk and XJDF also standardize return status notifications from the print provider back to the submitting storefront or ERP system. Using a PrintTalk `OrderStatusResponse` message, the MIS can report **XJDF Milestones**—a standardized mechanism “to track certain overall milestones concerning the entire job across all resources and processes in order to display this to the operator” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. 7.10.3.3 "Milestone"] (or to the customer in an online order portal)—as well as shipping details such as carrier tracking numbers via `DeliveryParams`.

The example below shows a PrintTalk `OrderStatusResponse` reporting the `"PressCompleted"` milestone for job `"Job-42"` (referencing the original order via `@BusinessRefID="CART-42"`):

```xml title="Example: Web-To-Print Status Feedback via PrintTalk and XJDF"
<ptk:PrintTalk xmlns:ptk="http://www.printtalk.org/schema_2_0"
        xmlns="http://www.CIP4.org/JDFSchema_2_0"
        Timestamp="2016-10-20T22:42:38Z" payloadID="fd891889" >
    <ptk:Request>
        <ptk:OrderStatusResponse BusinessID="CART-43"
                BusinessRefID="CART-42">
            <Notification JobID="Job-42" Class="Event">
                <Milestone MilestoneType="PressCompleted" />
            </Notification>
        </ptk:OrderStatusResponse>
    </ptk:Request>
</ptk:PrintTalk>
```

The table below lists the standard XJDF `@MilestoneType` values relevant to Web-To-Print status tracking in chronological order:

| MilestoneType | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `DigitalArtArrived` | “Digital content has been received” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: DigitalArtArrived]. Emitted once all digital artwork files have been transferred to the print provider. |
| `PrePressInProgress` | “At least one of the Prepress worksteps of the job is in progress status” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: PrePressInProgress]. Emitted as soon as prepress processing begins on the first part of the job. |
| `PrePressCompleted` | “All Prepress worksteps of the job have been completed. [...] In conventional prepress, this is the case when all plates have been made” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: PrePressCompleted]. |
| `PressInProgress` | “At least one of the Press worksteps of the job is in progress status” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: PressInProgress]. Emitted when printing begins on the press. |
| `PressCompleted` | “All Press worksteps of the job have been completed” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: PressCompleted]. Emitted once the final press run of the job has finished printing. |
| `PostPressInProgress` | “At least one of the Postpress worksteps of the job is in progress status” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: PostPressInProgress]. Emitted when postpress finishing begins. |
| `PostPressCompleted` | “All Postpress worksteps including packing of the job have been completed” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: PostPressCompleted]. Emitted once all finishing and packaging steps are complete. |
| `ShippingInProgress` | “Final product is being shipped” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: ShippingInProgress]. Emitted when the shipment is handed over to the carrier; may be accompanied by a `DeliveryParams` resource providing carrier tracking numbers, package counts, and weight. |
| `ShippingCompleted` | “Final product was delivered to the customer or distributors” [@xjdf_ip1, chap. A Table A.51: ShippingCompleted]. Emitted upon confirmed delivery. |
