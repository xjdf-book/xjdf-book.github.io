The CIP4 Organization is responsible for developing and maintaining open data exchange standards for the graphic arts industry. Because industry standards thrive on transparency, broad consensus, and active community participation, the CIP4 Authoring Process is designed to be open to everyone—including non-members—who wishes to follow or contribute to improvements and extensions of CIP4's specifications.

Anyone can register a free **visitor account** on the CIP4 website to access working group spaces and participate in technical discussions with the community. A visitor account can be upgraded to a full CIP4 member account at any time.

All specification work is tracked publicly in the **CIP4 JIRA System** (*https://jira.cip4.org*), powered by Atlassian JIRA (*https://www.atlassian.com*). Every proposed change follows a structured workflow from initial discussion to final release, and each CIP4 specification is managed in its own dedicated JIRA project:

| Specification | JIRA Project Link |
| --- | --- |
| XJDF Specification | https://jira.cip4.org/projects/XJDF |
| JDF Specification | https://jira.cip4.org/projects/JDF |
| PrintTalk Specification | https://jira.cip4.org/projects/PTK |
| ... | ... |

Every JIRA issue represents a **bug** (such as an editorial typo or technical inconsistency), an **improvement** (making the specification clearer or easier to use), or a **new feature/extension** (adding new attributes, elements, or process types). All specification projects are publicly readable without logging in, while registered CIP4 visitors and members can create new issues, comment on proposals, watch issues for updates, and vote on issues to help prioritize upcoming work. Detailed documentation on using CIP4's tools is available on the **CIP4 Confluence** wiki (*https://confluence.cip4.org*), which also hosts a public Q&A forum for technical and organizational questions.

## Specification Authoring Workflow
Every JIRA issue progresses through a standardized eight-stage workflow, illustrated in the diagram below:

### Status: Discussion
**Discussion** is the initial state of every newly created issue (and any issue reopened from a later state). Issues in `"Discussion"` are open for community review to clarify requirements and weigh pros and cons. Registered users (both members and visitors) are encouraged to share feedback and implementation experience in the issue comments, and open issues are reviewed regularly during public CIP4 Working Group web conferences. Once a consensus is reached during a working group meeting, the issue either moves forward to **Drafting** or is closed as **Rejected**.

### Status: Rejected
Issues that the working group decides not to pursue are transitioned to **Rejected** with an explanatory comment documenting the rationale. Archiving rejected proposals in JIRA prevents repeating the same debates in the future, while still allowing a rejected issue to be reopened into **Discussion** if new requirements emerge.

### Status: Drafting
Once a proposal is approved in principle, the exact wording and table changes for the specification are prepared in the JIRA issue's **Draft** field:

<figure>
  <Image src={screenJiraDraft} alt="Draft in JIRA" />
  <figcaption>Figure: Draft in JIRA</figcaption>
</figure>

In the **Draft** field, deletions are marked in red strikethrough text, additions are highlighted in green, and unchanged context remains in black. Once the draft text is complete, the issue advances to **TSC Approve**.

### Status: TSC Approve
CIP4's **Technical Steering Committee (TSC)** “is comprised of the chair people and alternate chairs of technical working groups and the Chief Technical Officer of CIP4. Chairmen of subgroups of working groups are also members of the TSC. [...] One objective of the TSC is to ensure that CIP4's specifications remain architecturally sound, and the TSC may reject working group recommendations if:

* A recommendation includes elements or functions that already exist, perhaps under different names, and would hence, create a redundancy
* A recommendation is in conflict with either existing specification features or features under development by one or more other working groups;
* A recommendation obviously requires implementation of essential IP, as defined in the CIP4 IP Policy, and the TSC knows that the essential IP will not be licensed by the owner
* A recommendation is technically unsound or questionable and requires further development
* A recommendation is outside of the scope of the originating working group and must be referred to the appropriate working group, and
* A recommendation is outside of the scope of the specification under development” [@cip4_tsc_2005].

In **TSC Approve**, the TSC reviews the proposed changes in the **Draft** field. If revisions are needed, the issue returns to **Drafting** or **Discussion** (or, in rare cases, **Rejected**). Once approved by the TSC, the issue moves to **Specification**.

### Status: Specification
In **Specification**, CIP4's specification editors incorporate the approved draft changes into the master specification source documents. CIP4 runs a continuous build pipeline for its specifications: as soon as a change is committed to the source repository, an updated draft PDF of the specification is automatically built and published to the TSC Spec Incubator space on CIP4 Confluence (*https://confluence.cip4.org/display/TSC/Spec+Incubator*). Once the specification text has been updated, the issue advances to **Schema** (`Scheme`).

### Status: Schema
Because CIP4's standards are XML formats, CIP4 publishes official XML Schema (XSD) files alongside the specification PDFs. In this stage, CIP4's schema editors apply any required XSD updates, which are likewise built continuously and published on CIP4 Confluence.

### Status: TSC Verify
Once both the specification document and the XML Schema have been updated, the TSC performs a final verification in **TSC Verify** to confirm that the edits match the approved draft. If any discrepancy is found, the issue returns to **Specification**; otherwise, it transitions to **Implemented**.

### Status: Implemented
**Implemented** is the final terminal state for completed issues. When a new version of a CIP4 specification is released, comprehensive release notes are generated directly from the **Implemented** issues in JIRA, providing a permanent, transparent audit trail of every change and its technical rationale.


## Meetings
In addition to asynchronous collaboration in JIRA, CIP4 working groups hold regular virtual and in-person meetings to discuss complex proposals in depth.

Each active CIP4 Working Group hosts a **public web conference** on a biweekly schedule. Anyone can join via a standard web browser with a headset. A core agenda item of every web conference is reviewing JIRA issues currently in **Discussion** and recording the outcome directly as a comment on each issue:

Comments recorded during working group meetings are tagged with a meeting hashtag (such as `#xjdf_20161110` in the screenshot above, indicating the XJDF Working Group meeting held on November 10, 2016). These hashtags allow Confluence to automatically aggregate all issues discussed during a session into the official meeting minutes. The schedule of upcoming web conferences and dial-in links can be found in the public CIP4 Calendar (*https://confluence.cip4.org/display/PUB/Calendar*).

### Face-to-Face Meetings
Several times a year, CIP4 also organizes in-person Face-to-Face (F2F) meetings—most notably the **CIP4 InterOp**, held twice a year: “These InterOp meetings are organized worldwide and are split into two distinct phases. The first phase, which typically lasts from Monday to Wednesday is the actual technical interoperability testing phase, where anyone can bring their JDF enabled product and test its interoperability with other matching products. The second phase lasts from Wednesday to Friday and consists of meetings of the respective technical work groups. Topics of discussion range from new features for the next specification version to over interpretation of individual attributes to philosophical debates about the value of namespace URIs in XML” [@cip4_interop_2016].

Dates, locations, and registration details for upcoming and past InterOp meetings are published on CIP4 Confluence (*https://confluence.cip4.org/display/PUB/InterOp*).


## ICS Development (Workgroups)
Individual CIP4 Working Groups are also responsible for developing and maintaining the **Interoperability Conformance Specifications (ICS Documents)** for their respective domains (such as Prepress, Conventional Printing, Digital Printing, or Finishing). Whenever an ICS working group identifies a need for a new element, attribute, or process type in the core XJDF Specification to support a Gray Box interface, it submits a JIRA issue into the XJDF Specification Authoring Workflow described above.
